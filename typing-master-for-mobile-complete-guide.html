What does typing master for mobile mean?

Typing Master is the name of well-known typing software for computers, and we are not connected with it. People who search for typing master for mobile want the same kind of experience on a phone: step-by-step lessons, timed tests and clear results. A good mobile typing tutor gives you three things.

Lessons

Lessons teach the keyboard in small steps. You start with the home row (A S D F and J K L ;), then the top row, the bottom row, capital letters, punctuation, numbers and symbols. Our free lessons follow this order in 15 stages and also include numpad, Roman Urdu and office typing.

Typing tests

A test measures your speed and accuracy. You choose a time from 1 to 20 minutes and a topic, type the passage and see your result. Tests are the best way to check if your lessons are working.

Games and drills

Games make short practice fun. Falling-word and 60 second games train your fingers to react quickly, and they keep working when you are offline.

How to start in 10 minutes

Step 1: Open it like an app

Open the website in Chrome and use Add to Home screen. The typing tutor then opens like an app and, after the first visit, can work without internet. You can also get the offline file from our download page.

Step 2: Take a baseline test

Take a 1 minute test first. Do not worry about the score. It simply tells you where you start, and you can compare it with the same test next week.

Step 3: Follow the lessons

Open Lessons and begin with Lesson 1. Finish each lesson with at least 95% accuracy before you move on. If a lesson feels hard, repeat it. Slow and clean typing builds speed faster than rushing.

Step 4: Practice every day

Ten to fifteen minutes a day is better than one long session a week. Muscle memory grows through repetition and sleep.

Phone typing and keyboard typing

Most phone users type with two thumbs, while computer typing uses ten fingers. Both skills matter. If your goal is a job test, practice on a real keyboard whenever you can.

Typing with your thumbs

Use landscape mode if your thumbs make many mistakes, because the keys are wider. Turn off autocorrect during practice so that you see your real mistakes.

Typing with a physical keyboard

You can connect a physical keyboard to your phone with an OTG adapter or Bluetooth. It feels like a computer and is very easy to set up. Read our guide to typing on mobile with an OTG keyboard.

How speed and accuracy are measured

Our tests show two speeds. Gross WPM counts every word you typed. Net WPM counts only the words that are fully correct, so a word with even one mistake does not count. Accuracy shows the percentage of keys you pressed correctly. For example, if you type 40 words in one minute and 4 of them contain a mistake, your gross speed is 40 WPM and your net speed is 36 WPM. Net speed is the number that matters for most jobs and exams, because it rewards clean typing.

A simple four-week plan

Week 1

Learn the home row and take one 1 minute test every day. Aim for 90% accuracy.

Week 2

Learn the top and bottom rows. Take 2 minute tests and aim for 95% accuracy.

Week 3

Practice capital letters, punctuation and numbers. Try a 5 minute test on a topic you like.

Week 4

Take 10 minute tests, play a typing game and compare your result with week 1.

Common mistakes to avoid

Looking at the keys, rushing, fixing every small error and skipping days are the four biggest habits that slow learners down. Keep your eyes on the screen, type at a steady rhythm and practice a little every day. Check your progress page to see which keys cause the most mistakes, and study our finger position chart.
